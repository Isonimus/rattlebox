## Goal

Add the D4 as the sixth die type, and with it the reading rule a tetrahedron forces. Every die
the product has shipped is centrally symmetric: each face has another exactly opposed to it, so
"the numeral on the face toward the glass" has been a complete rule for four slices. A
tetrahedron has no opposite face. It comes to rest on one face with the other three sloping up to
an apex, and the numeral a player reads is the one at that apex, which belongs to the face the
die is resting on and cannot be seen.

Two pieces of the product break on that solid rather than merely bending. `upFace` returns a
sloping face at 70.5288 degrees from the glass, which `updateResult` compares against
`COCKED_TILT_RAD` of 0.5 degrees, so a D4 would be declared cocked and nudged forever and would
never lock a result. And the three sloping faces point at the camera at exactly a third, under
the renderer's `NUMERAL_MIN_FACING` of 0.5, so a resting D4 would be drawn with no numerals on it
at all. Both are measured in `scripts/tetrahedron-probe.mjs`, not reasoned about.

This slice also closes the `[bug]` ledger item from ADR-0031: `scripts/fairness-probe.mjs` prints
each die's final tilt gap but never fails on it, so `COCKED_TILT_RAD` has been enforced by
somebody reading a summary. A die that changes what "lying flat" measures is the wrong moment to
leave that unenforced, so the probe gains a failure condition on the tilt gap in this slice.

## Definition of Done

- **Given** the tetrahedron probe
- **When** it measures the solid it builds
- **Then** it reports 4 vertices, 4 triangular faces, an isotropic inertia agreeing with the
  closed form of an edge squared over 20, and each vertex opposite exactly one face

- **Given** the shape limits raised for the D12
- **When** the D4 is added
- **Then** `DIE_MAX_VERTICES`, `DIE_MAX_FACES` and `FACE_MAX_CORNERS` are unchanged and neither
  board's static RAM grows by more than the catalog row itself

- **Given** a D4 that has come to rest flat on a face
- **When** its result locks
- **Then** the numeral is that resting face's, not any face toward the glass

- **Given** a D4 drawn at any orientation
- **When** the renderer places its numerals
- **Then** every visible face containing the apex vertex shows the same digit, and that digit is
  the result the band shows once the die locks

- **Given** a D4 propped against a wall or another die
- **When** it stops moving
- **Then** it reads as cocked and is nudged, and no result locks

- **Given** 2,000 traced D4 rolls through the real physics
- **When** the longest pause before a die still tipped onto another face is measured at
  `REST_ANGULAR_RAD_PER_S`
- **Then** the D4's `holdS` is four times it by ADR-0028's rule, and the die's mean roll is of the
  same order as the other five

- **Given** 2,000 traced rolls of each of the six dice
- **When** the probe compares the flattest propped die against the most tilted flat one
- **Then** it fails if that gap does not straddle `COCKED_TILT_RAD`, rather than printing it

- **Given** the fairness test
- **When** 1,900 D4 results are tallied one die at a time and 634 rolls three at a time, over
  three seeds each
- **Then** chi-square against a flat distribution and the share landing on the previous face both
  pass at alpha 0.001

- **Given** the die type control
- **When** the player cycles types
- **Then** the D4 comes first, before the D6, and the boot choice is still one D20

- **Given** a flashed Cardputer ADV and a flashed M5StickC Plus 2
- **When** `npm run verify:roll` rolls three D4 forty times on each
- **Then** every roll locks, no frame runs late, chi-square passes, and the saved capture shows
  three legible apex numerals matching the band

## Design

**The solid.** `scripts/tetrahedron-probe.mjs` measures it, reusing the integrator and the
self-check from `scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs` and the convex hull face search from
`scripts/dodecahedron-probe.mjs`. The vertices are alternate corners of a cube of side 2, which
puts the centroid at the origin exactly rather than to rounding. At circumradius 1 the probe
measures an inertia per unit mass of 0.1333333333 about every axis, off-diagonal terms at
1.39e-18, agreeing with the closed form of an edge squared over 20 to 2.78e-17. The circumradius
is 0.6123724357 of the edge, the square root of three eighths; the inradius is a third of the
circumradius; the dihedral angle is 70.5288 degrees. Sized to the D20's circumradius of 9.98609
mm, as every die since the D8 has been so the set shares one bounding sphere, the D4's edge is
16.307222 mm and its `inertiaPerMass` is 13.296274 mm squared.

**The reading rule.** The probe measures the fact the rule rests on: each vertex is opposite
exactly one face, and misses being exactly opposed by 0.00e+0 radians. That is not a tolerance,
it is an identity of the solid, and it means the apex standing over a resting die is the vertex
belonging to no corner of the face it rests on. So the result is the resting face's numeral, and
the renderer can place that numeral at the apex without the shape carrying a vertex-to-face table
at all: the face read is the one whose normal is most opposed to the glass, and the apex is the
vertex most aligned with it, and on a tetrahedron those two are the same statement.

This is why the rule is a flag and not a table. `DieShape` gains one enumerated field saying
where the die's result is read, in the same spirit as `holdS`, which ADR-0028 made a property of
the die rather than of the thresholds. A per-vertex numeral array was considered and rejected: at
20 bytes in every `DieShape`, it would charge all six dice for a fact only the D4 has, which is
precisely the cost ADR-0030 weighed and the reason the limits are not being raised again.

**What the renderer draws.** A face shows the apex numeral when it faces the camera *and*
contains the apex vertex. That is an exact geometric condition with no threshold in it, which
matters because `NUMERAL_MIN_FACING` of 0.5 would otherwise hide every numeral on a resting D4,
whose sloping faces face the camera at exactly a third. Lowering that threshold globally was
rejected: it governs when a numeral still reads on a small face and has nothing to do with the
D4's problem, so moving it would change the other five dice to fix this one. The numeral sits
between the apex's projected position and the face centre, so the digit is inside the triangle
rather than on its corner.

**What was considered and not reused.** `upFace` in `src/physics/die_result.cpp` is kept and
generalised rather than duplicated: finding the face most aligned with a direction is now one
function, called with the direction toward the glass for five dice and away from it for the D4.
Two call sites is not the rule of three, but this is not speculative generality: both callers
exist in this slice, and the alternative is the same loop written twice with one sign different,
which is the shape of bug a reader cannot see. The nudge path, the hold, the lock and the
fairness harness are reused unchanged; none of them asks which face is up, only whether the die
is flat.

**Fairness sizing.** By the same calculation as every die before it, 99% power against one face
1.25 times too likely at alpha 0.001: the non-centrality is 0.0625 times the results over the
degrees of freedom, and at 3 degrees of freedom the critical value is 16.266 and 1,821 results
reach the power. Rounded up as its neighbours were, the D4 is tallied over **1,900 results**, and
634 rolls of three dice. The calculation was checked by reproducing the five shipped sizes from
it before it was used for a new one.

**Where the D4 sits.** `DieType` is ordered by ascending face count so the cycle steps through it
in that order, so the D4 becomes the first entry and `DIE_TYPE_COUNT` becomes 6. `BOOT_CHOICE`
names its type rather than indexing the enum, so the device still boots on one D20.

## Verification

- `test/test_geometry` asserts the D4's vertex and face counts, that every face is wound
  counter-clockwise seen from outside, its circumradius, its isotropic inertia, and that each
  vertex is antipodal to one face. Scenario 1.
- `scripts/tetrahedron-probe.test.mjs` tests the probe's own arithmetic, including its self-check
  against solids whose inertia is known in closed form, the apex relation, and that the raised
  faces face the camera below the shipped numeral threshold. Scenario 1.
- Both boards' build output, compared against the figures slice 0029 recorded. Scenario 2.
- `test/test_die_result` asserts that a D4 at rest reads its resting face, that the same die read
  by the old top-face rule would not, and that a propped D4 reads as cocked. Scenarios 3 and 5.
- `test/test_render` asserts that a D4's visible faces containing the apex all carry the resting
  face's numeral, that a face not containing the apex carries none, and that the digit falls
  inside the drawn triangle. Scenario 4.
- `scripts/fairness-probe.mjs`, which traces 2,000 rolls of each die, derives each hold by
  ADR-0028's rule and now fails when the tilt gap does not straddle `COCKED_TILT_RAD`.
  Scenarios 6 and 7.
- `test/test_fairness` through `npm test` one die at a time and `npm run test:fairness` three at a
  time, at the sizes above. Scenario 8.
- `test/test_dice_choice` asserts the cycle order and the boot choice. Scenario 9.
- `npm run verify:roll -- --env <env> --port <port>` on each board, whose saved captures are read
  by eye. Scenario 10.

## As built

The D4 shipped as the sixth die type, read at its apex, and all ten Definition-of-Done scenarios
were met. One thing did not go as designed, and the device is what caught it; that is recorded
with the rest below rather than tidied away.

**The solid (scenario 1).** `scripts/tetrahedron-probe.mjs` measured a tetrahedron at
circumradius 1 with 4 vertices, 4 triangular faces and an inertia per unit mass of 0.1333333333
about every axis, off-diagonal terms at 1.39e-18, agreeing with the closed form of an edge
squared over 20 to 2.78e-17. The circumradius came out at 0.6123724357 of the edge, the square
root of three eighths; the inradius at a third of the circumradius; the dihedral angle at
70.5288 degrees. At the D20's circumradius of 9.98609 mm the die's edge is 16.307222 mm, the
largest in the set, and its `inertiaPerMass` is 13.296274 mm squared.

The measurement the whole slice rests on is the apex relation: each vertex is opposite exactly
one face, each face used once, missing exactly opposed by **0.00e+0 radians**. It is an identity
of the solid rather than a tolerance, which is why the shape carries no vertex-to-face table.
`test/test_geometry` checks the body closes, winds outward, has one edge length and one inradius,
and that no two of its faces are opposed at all, which is the property that forced the new rule.

**What it cost (scenario 2).** `DIE_MAX_VERTICES`, `DIE_MAX_FACES` and `FACE_MAX_CORNERS` are
untouched at 20, 20 and 5: 4 vertices, 4 faces and 3 corners all fit inside the ceiling ADR-0030
set, as that ADR predicted they would. Static RAM grew by **exactly 768 bytes on both boards**,
which is the catalog row and nothing else. The Cardputer is at 16.2% RAM (53,236 of 327,680 B)
and 14.8% flash (494,779 of 3,342,336 B); the StickC at 16.1% RAM (52,728 B) and 13.5% flash
(450,215 B).

**Reading the result (scenarios 3 and 5).** `DieShape` gained a `NumeralReading` field and
`readFace` dispatches on it, with the five shipped dice declaring `TopFace` and the D4 `Apex`.
The face search became one function taking a direction, called toward the glass for five dice and
toward the floor for the D4, rather than the same loop written twice with one sign changed.
`readFace` aborts on a value outside the enum rather than falling back to the top-face rule,
because a zeroed `DieShape` reads as `TopFace` and that is exactly the corruption a fallback
would hide. `test/test_die_result` asserts that a D4 at rest reads the face it is resting on for
all four faces, that the same pose measures 70.5 degrees away by the old rule against the 0.5
degree tolerance, that a propped D4 stays cocked through twice its hold, and that the other dice
read the same face they always did.

**What the screen shows, and the one deviation (scenario 4).** The rule shipped as designed: a
face carries the digit when it faces the camera and contains the raised corner, with no angle
threshold, so `NUMERAL_MIN_FACING` of 0.5 does not hide the sloping faces that face the camera at
exactly a third. The raised corner is found from the resting face, so the screen cannot disagree
with the band.

**Where the digit sits did not ship as designed.** The Design above says the numeral sits part of
the way from the apex toward the face centre, and it was built at half way on the reasoning that
the three copies should cluster and read as one number. On the Cardputer they did not cluster,
they collided: the first capture shows three overlapping marks instead of a digit. Measured at
the product's scale afterwards, a resting D4 is 45.65 px across and its apex projects to the
middle of its own silhouette, so half way leaves the closest pair of digits **6.21 px** apart
against a glyph 6 px wide and 8 px tall. At the face centres they are **12.42 px** apart and
clear in both axes. The digit now sits at the face centre, which is where every other die's
numeral sits, so the D4 differs only in which numeral each face carries. The measurement is
written at the code site, because the corner is where a printed D4 writes and a later reader will
reasonably want to move it back. What a 45 px die with a 6 px font cannot do is write three
numbers near one corner, and that is a fact about the screen rather than about the design.

**The hold and the threshold under it (scenario 6).** Over 2,000 traced rolls the D4's longest
pause before it still tipped was **0.0042 s** at the product's 5 mm/s and 0.1 rad/s, so ADR-0028's
rule gives a hold of 0.02 s. That is the shortest pause of the dice that pause at all, against
0.0083 s for the D12 and the D20 and 0.0167 s for the D10; only the D6 and the D8 sit at the
rule's 0.01 s floor, having shown no false rest. The first probe run was made at that floor and
failed,
naming the die and the number, which is the check working rather than a surprise. The trace grid
shows ADR-0031's lesson again from a new direction: the same die pauses 0.021 s at 1 rad/s and
0.046 s at 2 rad/s, so almost all of what a looser threshold would have made the D4 wait out is a
die still turning.

**The tilt gap, now enforced (scenario 7).** This slice closed the `[bug]` ledger item ADR-0031
opened. The probe program prints the cocked tolerance it locks by on its trace grid, and
`checkTiltGap` fails when a die's measured gap does not straddle it: a flat die at or past the
tolerance would be nudged for ever, a propped die at or under it would lock while cocked. Both
are silent failures, and until now both were caught only by somebody reading a summary. All six
dice pass. The D4's gap is the widest in the set, flat up to 0.0485 degrees and propped from
**24.8823 degrees**, because a propped tetrahedron leans harder than any other die: the one place
this die makes the thresholds safer rather than tighter. The D20's remains the narrowest at
0.0626 against 0.6775, and it is now a test failure rather than a line to notice.

**Fairness (scenario 8).** 1,900 results one die at a time and 634 rolls of three, over three
seeds each, sized by the same calculation as every die before it: 99% power against one face
1.25 times too likely at alpha 0.001, which at 3 degrees of freedom needs 1,821 results against a
critical 16.266. The calculation was checked by reproducing the five shipped sizes from it before
it was trusted for a new one. Chi-square came out between 0.49 and 5.04 across the six tallies,
p from 0.169 to 0.920, with no roll left unsettled and nothing knocked over after locking. The
mean three-dice roll is 0.37 s, which puts the D4 beside the D6 as the quickest die in the set.

**Choosing the die (scenario 9).** `DieType` is ordered by ascending face count, so the D4 became
the first entry and `DIE_TYPE_COUNT` is 6. `BOOT_CHOICE` names its type rather than indexing the
enum, so the device still boots on one D20. The cycle now runs D4, D6, D8, D10, D12, D20 and back.

**On the devices (scenario 10).** Both boards were flashed and ran `npm run verify:roll`, 40
rolls of three D4 each among the six types, and both passed with **no late frame anywhere**.

| | Cardputer ADV | M5StickC Plus 2 |
|---|---|---|
| D4 chi-square (p) | 5.67 (0.129) | 3.27 (0.352) |
| Rolls never settled | 0 | 0 |
| Mean / longest roll | 0.38 / 0.55 s | 0.38 / 0.55 s |
| Nudges over 120 results | 5 | 4 |
| Frame work, mean / longest | 10,206 / 11,935 us | 4,047 / 6,899 us |
| Four physics steps, longest | 3,001 us | 2,926 us |
| Late frames | 0 | 0 |

The D4 is the cheapest die the product simulates, at 2,926 us of physics on the StickC against
the D12's 5,760, because a tetrahedron has four vertices to collide where a dodecahedron has
twenty. Its worst StickC frame left 9.8 ms of the 16,667 us budget unused.

The captures were read by eye, which is the half of scenario 10 no host can answer. The Cardputer
shows three D4 reading 1, 2 and 3 against a band of `D4 1 2 3 = 6`, and the StickC three reading
4 against `D4 4 4 4 = 12`. On both, each die carries three separated, legible copies of its own
result, and the Cardputer's capture is the one in `README.md`.

**A note for whoever adds the d%.** Standing invariant #7 now makes a die declare where its result
is read, and `test/test_geometry` checks that declaration against the solid for every catalog
entry. A d% reads faces and declares `TopFace`, so it inherits the check for free; what it will
have to answer instead is how a units die reads its 10 back as 0, which is a numbering question
this slice did not touch.
