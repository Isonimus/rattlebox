## Goal

The third of v0.2's dice (`docs/roadmap.md`): a D12, rolled one to three at a time like the D6,
the D8, the D10 and the D20, and passing invariant #2's fairness test before it ships (ADR-0002,
ADR-0009). Unlike the three dice before it, the D12 does not fit the body `die_shape.h` describes:
a dodecahedron has 20 vertices against `DIE_MAX_VERTICES = 12` and pentagons against
`FACE_MAX_CORNERS = 4`. Those two limits size arrays and loop bounds that every die pays for, so
raising them is a change to the D6 as much as to the D12, and that decision is ADR-0030's rather
than this slice's. The geometry itself is the easiest of v0.2's dice: a dodecahedron is Platonic,
so its inertia is isotropic without a proportion having to be chosen for it, as the D10's did.
No ledger item bears on it.

## Definition of Done

- **Given** the dodecahedron `makeD12` builds
- **When** the geometry test checks it
- **Then** it is closed and wound outward, it has 20 vertices and 12 five-cornered faces, every
  vertex is the same distance from the centre and every face the same distance from it, every
  face's normal is exactly opposed by another face's, its numerals run 1 to 12 with opposite faces
  summing to 13, and its inertia is isotropic and matches the integrated body about all three axes

- **Given** `DIE_MAX_VERTICES` rises from 12 to 20 and `FACE_MAX_CORNERS` from 4 to 5
- **When** both boards are built and the fairness test is run for the D6, the D8, the D10 and the
  D20
- **Then** every die already shipped tallies and locks as it did before, no board overflows its
  RAM, and the cost of the raise is the measured one recorded in ADR-0030

- **Given** one D12, then three at a time, rolled one roll after another on the recorded shakes
- **When** the fairness test tallies 9,000 results
- **Then** chi-square stays under 31.264 (11 degrees of freedom, α = 0.001), the share of results
  on the die's previous face passes against 1 in 12, and every roll comes to rest

- **Given** each die carries its own lock hold, measured from its own trace (ADR-0028)
- **When** the fairness probe traces 2,000 D12 rolls
- **Then** the D12's hold is the next hundredth of a second strictly above four times the longest
  pause after which a D12 still tipped, and the flattest propped D12 tilts more than
  `COCKED_TILT_RAD` while the most tilted flat one tilts less; if the tilt fails, the threshold is
  re-chosen from the measured numbers before the D12 ships

- **Given** the angular rest speed drops from 0.5 to 0.1 rad/s (ADR-0031), so every die's hold is
  re-measured
- **When** the fairness probe traces and tallies all five dice and the three-dice fairness test
  runs
- **Then** every die's hold outlasts its own traced pause four times over, no roll is left
  unsettled, every die's propped tilt still clears 0.5° while its flat tilt does not, and every
  die locks sooner than it did

- **Given** any choice of die type
- **When** PRIMARY is pressed (Enter on the Cardputer, button A on the StickC)
- **Then** the type steps D6, D8, D10, D12, D20 and back to D6, and the count stays as it was

- **Given** a board flashed with this build
- **When** the D12 is picked and the device is shaken
- **Then** the band reads `D12` before the roll, the dice tumble and each lands showing the numeral
  the band reports for it, with the numeral legible on a pentagon including the two-digit 10, 11
  and 12, and the frame budget is kept: no late frames on either board

## Design

**Shape.** A regular dodecahedron: 20 vertices, 12 regular pentagons, 5 corners to a face, one
edge length and one dihedral angle of 116.565°. It is built from the three golden rectangles and
the cube they inscribe, the construction whose coordinates are exact in terms of φ, rather than by
solving for a pentagon's corners numerically.

**What it costs every other die, which is the one thing this slice had to measure.** 20 vertices
and 5 corners do not fit `die_shape.h`'s limits, and those limits are not merely a maximum: they
size `ContactMemory`, `world.cpp`'s contact array, `HitFinder::_trayNormal` and `DieShape` itself,
and `src/sound/hit_finder.cpp` deliberately scans the whole of `DIE_MAX_VERTICES` rather than each
die's own count. Measured rather than estimated, by building both boards at 12/4 and again at
20/5, and by timing the product's own `stepWorld` and `HitFinder::find` natively at `-O2`:

| | Cardputer | StickC Plus 2 |
|---|---|---|
| RAM at 12 vertices, 4 corners | 43,284 B (13.2%) | 42,760 B (13.0%) |
| RAM at 20 vertices, 5 corners | 51,732 B (15.8%) | 51,208 B (15.6%) |
| Flash added | 396 B | 384 B |

The CPU cost of the raise, per frame of four physics steps, two runs of each configuration:

| Die | vertices | at 12/4 | at 20/5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| D6 | 8 | 63.5 / 65.3 µs | 64.5 / 65.6 µs |
| D8 | 6 | 45.5 / 46.3 µs | 45.8 / 46.3 µs |
| D10 | 12 | 59.1 / 59.3 µs | 60.8 / 59.5 µs |
| D20 | 12 | 53.6 / 54.0 µs | 54.5 / 54.2 µs |
| D12 | 20 | n/a | 78.2 / 76.5 µs |

The raise costs the dice already shipped under 2%, which is inside this host's own run-to-run
spread and so is not resolvable; what the measurement establishes is the bound, not a figure. The
D12 itself costs **1.42× the D20** per frame. The host predicts the device: it puts the D10 at
1.10× the D20 and the device measured 4.4 ms against 4.0 ms, the same ratio, so the D12 should sit
near 5.7 ms of the StickC's 16.67 ms frame, and it draws 12 faces where a D20 draws 20. The
decision to take the raise globally, and the option measured and rejected, are ADR-0030's.

**Why it is fair.** The probe measures what the symmetry argument predicts: all twelve faces lie
the same distance from the centre (spread exactly 0 in double arithmetic), all twenty vertices the
same distance, and the solid is face-transitive, so no face is geometrically privileged. Being
Platonic, its inertia is isotropic with no proportion to choose: 0.3093188789 R² about all three
axes, with off-diagonal terms at 7.5 × 10⁻¹⁸. `DieShape`'s single `inertiaPerMass` scalar is
therefore exact for it, as it is for the D6, the D8 and the D20, and the contact solver is
untouched. The fairness test measures it rather than taking the argument on trust.

**Size.** Circumradius 9.9861 mm, the D20's, as the D8 and the D10 took it (slices 0026 and 0027),
so the dice of a set share one bounding sphere and dice-to-dice contact behaves as it already
does. A dodecahedron's circumradius is 1.4012585 of its edge, so `makeD12` takes an edge of
7.1265 mm like the other regular builders rather than a circumradius like `makeD10`, which needed
one only because a kite has two edge lengths. That gives inertiaPerMass 30.8459 mm².

**Numbering.** Faces carry 1 to 12, with opposite faces summing to 13, which is what
`DieFace::numeral` already requires of every die and what the probe confirms is geometrically
meaningful here: each face's normal is exactly opposed by another's (to 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶) and the
pairing is an involution, so "opposite" is well defined and the face lying flattest on top reads
the result through the existing `upFace`. Unlike the D10 there is nothing to decide: a printed D12
reads 1 to 12 and so does this one.

**Fairness sizing.** Slice 0008's method, reproduced: one face 1.25× too likely with the others
sharing the shortfall, 99% power at α = 0.001 from the noncentral chi-square. In closed form the
non-centrality is 0.0625 N / (faces − 1). The method returns 3,366, 5,083, 6,944 and 17,937 for
the dice already shipped at 3,400, 5,100, 7,000 and 18,000, so it is the same calculation; for 12
faces it gives 8,932, and the D12 test takes **9,000 results** against a critical value of
**31.264** at 11 degrees of freedom, which is 3,000 rolls of three dice.

**The lock hold, and the rest threshold it uncovered.** Measured from the D12's own trace by
ADR-0028's rule, not argued. Its single dihedral angle of 116.565° falls between the D10's 97.96°
and 124.41° and the D20's 138.2°, which suggested a hold between 0.16 s and 0.21 s. The
measurement refused that, and ADR-0028 is explicit that a hold cannot be chosen by argument from
the dihedral angle however well the ordering fits.

What the trace found was one roll in 2,000 pausing **0.1583 s**, against 0.0500 s for the worst
die before it, which the four-times rule turns into a 0.64 s hold and a 1.451 s mean roll: double
every other die. The same roll's pause is **0.0000 s at 0.1 rad/s**, so the die was never still.
It was creeping over an edge slowly enough to stay under the 0.5 rad/s rest threshold while still
turning, and the hold had been waiting out a rotation rather than a balance, on every die.
**ADR-0031 drops the angular rest speed to 0.1 rad/s and re-measures all five holds**, which is
why this slice changes the four dice already shipped. `COCKED_TILT_RAD` stays at 0.5° and is
checked against every die's trace again.

This is the larger half of the slice, and it was not foreseen when the slice was written. It is
kept here rather than split out because the D12 cannot ship without it: at 0.64 s the die is not
one anybody would want, and the threshold is the root cause rather than a separate improvement.

**What existing code was considered.** `scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs` already holds an exact
inertia integrator, an inradius and opposite-face check, edge and dihedral measures, and a
self-check against a cube and an icosahedron that passes to 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶. All of that is reused by
importing it; what is not reused is the file itself, whose prose is about choosing one member of a
one-parameter family and whose `measure` takes that family's parameter. A dodecahedron has no
family, so it gets `scripts/dodecahedron-probe.mjs` beside it rather than a `t` argument that
would mean nothing. The new probe checks its measured inertia against the closed form
(39φ + 28)/150 of the edge squared, which is an independent route to the same number.
`makeD20`'s `triangleFace` and `makeD6`'s quadrilateral helper both take a fixed corner count, so
`makeD12` adds a five-corner helper rather than generalising either, and `makeD10`'s `kiteFace`
stays as it is. The renderer needs no change: `src/render/die_drawing.cpp` already fans a triangle
from corner 0 across any convex polygon, and a regular pentagon's corner mean is its centroid, so
the caveat recorded at `faceCentre` for the D10's kites does not apply here. `upFace` in
`src/physics/die_result.cpp` is unchanged. `test_geometry`'s `assertRegularSolid` does apply,
because the D12 has one edge length and one circumradius, so the D12 takes the stricter assertion
rather than the isohedral one slice 0027 added for the D10.

## Verification

- `scripts/dodecahedron-probe.mjs`: measures the solid and prints the constants the firmware
  needs, after reproducing a cube's and an icosahedron's known inertia to prove its arithmetic and
  checking its own result against the closed form. A probe, so not wired into `package.json`
  (CLAUDE.md section 3). Covers the first Definition-of-Done scenario's inertia and the design's
  measured geometry; `scripts/dodecahedron-probe.test.mjs` covers the probe itself.
- `test/test_geometry`: `makeD12` is closed, wound outward, regular, isohedral, numbered with
  opposite faces summing to 13, and its `inertiaPerMass` matches the body integrated about all
  three axes. Covers the first scenario.
- `test/test_fairness`: 9,000 D12 results against 31.264, one die at a time in `npm test` and
  three at a time under `npm run test:fairness`, plus the repeat-face share. Covers the third
  scenario, and with the other dice's cases the second.
- `scripts/fairness-probe.mjs`: the trace grid for the D12, from which its hold is read and
  against which `COCKED_TILT_RAD` is checked, and which fails a die whose hold does not outlast
  its longest false rest four times over. Covers the fourth scenario.
- `test/test_dice_choice`: the type cycles D6, D8, D10, D12, D20 and back, and the count is
  untouched. Covers the fifth scenario.
- `npm run verify:roll` on both boards, 40 rolls of three D12s among the rest: every roll locks,
  every type passes chi-square, and the artifacts carry the captures and the frame timings for the
  sixth scenario, which is the one only a device can answer.
- Both boards build (invariant #1), and `npm run test:fairness` runs before merge because the
  shape limits are physics input (invariant #2).

## As built

All seven Definition-of-Done scenarios were met. The slice grew one scenario while it was open:
the D12's own hold measurement uncovered a fault in the angular rest threshold that every die had
been paying for, and the fifth scenario was added for it. That is recorded in ADR-0031 and is the
larger half of what shipped.

**The geometry (scenario 1).** `makeD12` builds the dodecahedron from the cube and the three
golden rectangles, as 10 vertices and 6 faces mirrored through the centre. `test_geometry` asserts
it through `assertRegularSolid`: closed, wound outward, 20 vertices, 12 five-cornered faces, one
circumradius, one inradius, every face normal exactly opposed by another's, numerals 1 to 12 with
opposite faces summing to 13, and `inertiaPerMass` matching the body integrated about all three
axes. `scripts/dodecahedron-probe.mjs` measured the constants and checked its own result against
the closed form (39 phi + 28)/150: 0.3093188789 of the edge squared, off-diagonal terms at
7.5e-18.

Writing that probe found a real bug in its own face search, which is the reason the slice has a
regression test for a script. `hullFaces` identified a face from a vertex triple taken in
ascending index order and discarded any plane that wound inward. For a convex polygon every such
triple winds the same way, so an ordering can exist in which all ten of a pentagon's triples wind
inward and the face is never seen: the half-plus-mirror order `die_shape.cpp` lists its vertices
in loses three of the twelve that way. The fix turns the plane outward rather than discarding it.
The test fails before it and passes after.

**What it cost every die (scenario 2).** `DIE_MAX_VERTICES` rose 12 to 20 and `FACE_MAX_CORNERS`
4 to 5, which ADR-0030 records as a cost every die pays. Measured, it is exactly 8,448 bytes of
static RAM on both boards: `ContactMemory` 3,600 to 5,904, the contact array 111 to 183 entries at
72 bytes, `HitFinder::_trayNormal` 576, and four `DieShape` rows 384. Both boards build and sit
far inside their RAM: the Cardputer at 16.0% and 14.7% of flash, the StickC Plus 2 at 15.9% and
13.4%. The CPU cost to the dice already shipped measured under 2%, inside the host's own
run-to-run spread, so the measurement bounds it rather than resolving it. Narrowing
`hit_finder.cpp` to each die's own vertex count was measured and rejected in ADR-0030, and the
reason is cited at the scan itself so it is not later "optimised".

**Fairness (scenario 3).** 9,000 D12 results against a critical value of 31.264 at 11 degrees of
freedom, one die at a time in `npm test` and three at a time under `npm run test:fairness`. Over
six runs the statistic came out between 5.89 and 11.58, p 0.396 to 0.880, with no roll left
unsettled. The repeat-face share passes against 1 in 12.

**The hold, and the threshold under it (scenarios 4 and 5).** The D12's trace gave a longest false
rest of 0.1583 s at the old 0.5 rad/s, which ADR-0028's rule turns into a 0.64 s hold and a 1.451 s
mean three-dice roll: double every other die. A control first confirmed the harness reproduced all
four shipped dice's recorded holds exactly, so the figure was the die's and not the tooling's. The
same roll's pause is 0.0000 s at 0.1 rad/s, which located the fault in the threshold rather than
the die: it was counting a slow tip as rest, and the holds had been growing to outlast a rotation.
ADR-0031 drops `REST_ANGULAR_RAD_PER_S` to 0.1 and re-measures all five holds, which collapse to
D6 0.01 s, D8 0.01 s, D10 0.07 s, D12 0.04 s and D20 0.04 s. Every die now locks sooner: the
three-dice mean roll falls 0.03 s for the D6, 0.18 s for the D10, 0.19 s for the D20 and 0.20 s
for the D8. `COCKED_TILT_RAD` stayed at 0.5 degrees and was re-checked against every die's trace;
the narrowest gap is the D20's, flat at 0.0626 degrees against propped at 0.6775. Freezing this
slice found that "checked" there means read by a person: the probe prints each die's tilt gap but
has no failure condition on it, unlike the hold, which it does enforce. The gap is in `LEDGER.md`
citing ADR-0031, and it is why the D20's narrowing margin is worth watching as dice are added.

**Choosing the die (scenario 6).** `test_dice_choice` asserts the type steps D6, D8, D10, D12, D20
and back to D6 with the count untouched.

**On the devices (scenario 7).** `npm run verify:roll` ran on both boards, 40 rolls of three D12s
among the rest, and passed: every roll locked, every type passed chi-square, and no board produced
a late frame. The D12 is the most expensive die, as predicted, and stays inside the 16,667 us
frame with room: on the StickC 7,470 us mean and 11,074 us at worst over 1,286 frames, its four
physics steps peaking at 6,049 us; on the Cardputer 13,066 us mean and 15,208 us at worst over
1,283 frames, physics peaking at 5,883 us. The host predicted about 5,700 us of physics from its
1.42x ratio against the D20 and measured 6,049, which is 6% low and the right size. The captures
show the numerals legible on a pentagon, two-digit ones included: the Cardputer read 10, 4 and 2
and the StickC 11, 6 and 12, each matching its results band.

One device run produced a D10 chi-square of 27.00 (p = 0.0014) over 120 results, with one face
landing 27 times against an expectation of 12. It was not dismissed: a second run of the same
board put that face at 12, dead on expectation, and a different die was the outlier instead, which
is what chance looks like rather than bias. Pooled over both runs nothing approaches the alpha
this check uses. The device check exists to catch a miscompile that pins every launch to one face,
not to establish fairness; fairness is the host test's 7,000 and 18,000 results against the
device's 240 and 360.
