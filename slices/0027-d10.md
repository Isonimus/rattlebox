## Goal

The second of v0.2's dice (`docs/roadmap.md`): a D10, rolled one to three at a time like the D6,
the D8 and the D20, and passing invariant #2's fairness test before it ships (ADR-0002, ADR-0009).
It is the first die that is not a Platonic solid, which is the whole of its difficulty: its faces
are kites rather than regular polygons, its vertices do not all lie on one sphere, and its inertia
is the same about every axis only at one proportion out of a family. It goes before the D12
because it needs no change to the physics and no change to `die_shape.h`'s limits, while the D12
needs 20 vertices against today's 12. No ledger item bears on it.

## Definition of Done

- **Given** the trapezohedron `makeD10` builds
- **When** the geometry test checks it
- **Then** it is closed and wound outward, it has 12 vertices and 10 four-cornered faces, every
  face is the same distance from the centre, every face's normal is exactly opposed by another
  face's, its numerals run 1 to 10 with opposite faces summing to 11, and its inertia is isotropic
  and matches the integrated body about all three axes

- **Given** the D10's one free proportion
- **When** `scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs` sweeps the family and reports the principal moments
- **Then** the proportion chosen is the one whose inertia tensor is isotropic, and the probe
  reproduces a cube's and an icosahedron's known inertia to prove its own arithmetic

- **Given** one D10, then three at a time, rolled one roll after another on the recorded shakes
- **When** the fairness test tallies 7,000 results
- **Then** chi-square stays under 27.877 (9 degrees of freedom, α = 0.001), the share of results
  on the die's previous face passes against 1 in 10, and every roll comes to rest

- **Given** the rest and cocked thresholds were measured on the D6, the D8 and the D20, whose
  dihedral angles are 90°, 109.5° and 138.2°
- **When** the fairness probe traces 2,000 D10 rolls, a die whose two dihedral angles are 97.96°
  and 124.41°
- **Then** the longest false rest, times four, stays under `REST_HOLD_S`, and the flattest propped
  D10 tilts more than `COCKED_TILT_RAD` while the most tilted flat one tilts less; if either
  fails, the threshold is re-chosen from the measured numbers before the D10 ships

- **Given** any choice of die type
- **When** PRIMARY is pressed (Enter on the Cardputer, button A on the StickC)
- **Then** the type steps D6, D8, D10, D20 and back to D6, and the count stays as it was

- **Given** a board flashed with this build
- **When** the D10 is picked and the device is shaken
- **Then** the band reads `D10` before the roll, the dice tumble and each lands showing the
  numeral the band reports for it, with the numeral legible on a kite

## Design

**Shape.** A pentagonal trapezohedron: two apexes on the z axis and two staggered rings of five,
making 12 vertices, 10 kite faces and 4 corners to a face. That is exactly `DIE_MAX_VERTICES`,
well inside `DIE_MAX_FACES`, and exactly `FACE_MAX_CORNERS`, so no limit in `die_shape.h` moves.
It is built as the dual of a pentagonal antiprism of half-height t, which makes every kite planar
by construction rather than by solving for planarity and hoping the root is the convex one.

**The proportion, and why it is the one thing this slice had to measure.** `DieShape` carries
inertia as a single `inertiaPerMass` scalar, because "a uniform Platonic solid's inertia is the
same about every axis", and `src/physics/world.cpp` uses that scalar directly in the contact
solver's `inverseInertia`, in the warm start and in `totalEnergy`. A trapezohedron is not
Platonic, so in general its inertia is a tensor and the scalar would be a lie the solver cannot
compensate for. Sweeping the family (`scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs`) shows the inertia ratio
I<sub>xx</sub>/I<sub>zz</sub> passing through 1, and at **t = 0.767463** the tensor is isotropic
to 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁸ on its off-diagonal terms, with I/m = 0.202229 R² about all three axes. At that
proportion the scalar is exact, not an approximation, and the physics is untouched.

That proportion is an ordinary-looking D10: height to width 1.179, kite edges 1.2329 R and
0.5653 R, apex-to-ring radius ratio 1.170.

Considered and not taken, both measured: the proportion at which every vertex lies on one sphere
(t = 0.8995), which keeps `DieShape::circumradius`'s promise exactly and gives a height-to-width
of 1.006, but spreads the inertia 10.0% about its mean; and teaching the physics a full inertia
tensor, which would free the proportion but rewrite the contact solver's effective mass, the warm
start and the integrator, add the ω × (Iω) term, and force the D6, the D8 and the D20 through a
fresh three-dice fairness run and fresh threshold traces. Between the two promises `DieShape`
makes, the circumradius is the one that survives being bent: it is the bounding sphere dice
collide by (ADR-0002), so taking it as the apex distance leaves it an upper bound, which is
conservative and still correct, while a wrong inertia is simply wrong. `DieShape::circumradius`'s
comment gains that distinction.

**Why it is fair.** The probe measures what the symmetry argument predicts: all ten faces lie the
same distance from the centre (spread 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶) and the solid is face-transitive, so no face is
geometrically privileged. With the inertia isotropic as well, the whole body is symmetric under
the rotations that permute its faces, which is the same ground the D6, D8 and D20 stand on. The
fairness test measures it rather than taking the argument on trust.

**Size.** Circumradius 10.0 mm, the D20's, as the D8 took it (slice 0026), so the dice of a set
share one bounding sphere and dice-to-dice contact behaves as it already does. `makeD10` therefore
takes a circumradius where the other builders take an edge length: a kite has two edge lengths, so
an edge is not a size for this die. That gives a die 20.0 mm tall and 17.0 mm wide, with
inertiaPerMass 20.2229 mm².

**Numbering.** Faces carry 1 to 10, with opposite faces summing to 11, which is what
`DieFace::numeral` already requires of every die and what the probe confirms is geometrically
meaningful here: each face's normal is exactly opposed by another's (to 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶) and the
pairing is an involution, so "opposite" is well defined and the face lying flattest on top reads
the result through the existing `upFace`. The five faces around the +z apex take 1 to 5 and each
face opposite takes 11 − n. Considered and not taken: numbering 0 to 9 as a physical D10 is
printed, which would make opposite faces sum to 9, break the one numbering rule every die shares,
and let three dice total 0. A physical D10's "0" is read as 10 at the table, and d% will read a
units die's 10 as 0 when it comes (`docs/roadmap.md`), which is a question for that slice.

**Fairness sizing.** Slice 0008's method, reproduced: one face 1.25× too likely with the others
sharing the shortfall, 99% power at α = 0.001 from the noncentral chi-square. It returns 3,366,
5,083 and 17,937 for the dice already shipped at 3,400, 5,100 and 18,000, so it is the same
calculation; for 10 faces it gives 6,944, and the D10 test takes **7,000 results** against a
critical value of **27.877** at 9 degrees of freedom.

**Thresholds measured on other dice.** `REST_HOLD_S` is four times the longest pause after which
a die still tipped, and the D8 already forced it from 0.15 s to 0.17 s (slice 0026's amendment to
ADR-0009). Neither it nor `COCKED_TILT_RAD` holds for a D10 by argument: the D10 has two dihedral
angles, 97.96° and 124.41°, the sharper of them sharper than any die so far, so it tips and props
unlike the others. The fairness probe's `trace` mode measures both again.

**What existing code was considered.** `makeD20`'s `triangleFace` builds triangles and is not
reusable for kites; `makeD6`'s square-face helper takes four corners and is the closer fit, so
`makeD10` reuses the quadrilateral path and supplies its own vertices. The die catalog added in
`src/roll/die_catalog.{h,cpp}` is where the D10 becomes selectable: one row, and the enum, the
band's name, the shape the physics rolls and the name the fairness probe's argument spells all
follow, which is what that refactor was for. `upFace` in `src/physics/die_result.cpp` reads the
face whose normal is most aligned with the glass and needs no change for a non-Platonic die. The
renderer places a numeral at the mean of a face's projected corners
(`src/render/die_view.cpp`), which for a kite is not the visual centre; whether it reads well is
a thing only the device capture can answer, so it is a Definition-of-Done scenario rather than a
code change made in advance. `test_geometry`'s `assertRegularSolid` does not apply, because the
D10 has neither one edge length nor one circumradius; it gains a sibling for an isohedral solid
that checks closure, winding, the single inradius, the opposed normals, the numerals and the
inertia, and the D6, D8 and D20 keep the stricter assertion.

## Verification

- `scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs`: sweeps the trapezohedron family and prints the principal moments,
  the inradius spread, the opposed-normal error, the dihedral angles and the edge lengths, and
  checks its own arithmetic against a cube's exact a²/6 and an icosahedron's φ²/10 before
  reporting. A probe answers its question once; it is not a verify script, so it is not wired into
  `package.json` (CLAUDE.md §3).
- `test/test_geometry`: the D10 passes the new isohedral-solid assertion, including that its
  inertia matches the integrated body about all three axes, not just one (`npm test`).
- `test/test_fairness`: one D10 at a time in `npm test`, three at a time in
  `npm run test:fairness` (invariant #2).
- `test/test_dice_choice`: PRIMARY steps D6, D8, D10, D20, D6.
- `test/test_die_catalog`: already walks every row, so the D10's row is checked for a name
  matching its face count without a new test.
- `scripts/fairness-probe.mjs` with `trace D10 2000 1`: the longest false rest and the flat and
  propped tilts, recorded in `## As built`.
- `scripts/roll-verify.mjs` on both boards (`npm run verify:roll`): three D10 rolled through the
  product's serial commands, so every roll must lock and the D10's on-device results pass its
  chi-square. Its screen capture after the D10 rolls is compared with the band by eye, which is
  also how the numeral-on-a-kite scenario is judged: a rendered numeral cannot be asserted in a
  unit test.

## As built

The D10 shipped as designed in its geometry and its numbering: a pentagonal trapezohedron built
as the dual of a pentagonal antiprism at **t = 0.767463**, the proportion whose inertia tensor is
isotropic, at the D20's 10.0 mm circumradius, numbered 1 to 10 with opposite faces summing to 11,
and chosen by one new row in the die catalog.

**The measured shape** (`node scripts/die-shape-probe.mjs`, which reproduces a cube's a²/6 and an
icosahedron's φ²/10 to 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ before reporting anything): I/m = 0.202229 R² about all three
axes with the largest off-diagonal term at 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁸; every face the same distance from the
centre to 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶; every face's normal exactly opposed by another's, the pairing an
involution; kite edges 1.232854 R and 0.565306 R; dihedral angles 97.9589° and 124.4124°; height
to width 1.179. `test/test_geometry` then checks the built body independently, including that its
inertia matches the integrated body about **all three** axes rather than z alone, which is the
assertion the single scalar actually rests on and which the other three dice now pass too.

Two things the design did not foresee, both in `finish()`'s assumptions rather than in the D10:
`DieShape::circumradius` was taken from vertex 0 under the comment "every vertex of a regular
solid is equally far from its centre", which is false for a D10 whose apexes reach 1.170 times as
far as its ring, so it is now the furthest vertex; and a bare `TWO_PI` in `die_shape.cpp` collided
with `launch.cpp`'s when a test includes both files and their anonymous namespaces merge.

**The hold did not survive the D10, and the rule changed rather than the number.** Over 2,000
traced D10 rolls the longest pause below 5 mm/s and 0.5 rad/s after which a die still tipped was
0.0500 s, which the 0.17 s hold does not outlast four times over. Raising the single hold again,
as slice 0026 did for the D8, would have put every die at 0.21 s, six times what a D6's own
0.0083 s pause calls for and about 170 ms of dead time on the most-rolled die, with the D4 and the
D12 still able to ratchet it further. **ADR-0028** makes the hold a property of the die, carried
on `DieShape` beside its inertia and measured from its own trace: D6 0.04 s, D20 0.16 s, D8 0.17 s
and D10 0.21 s. `RestThresholds` lost its `holdS`, which left `PRODUCT_REST_THRESHOLDS` and all of
its call sites unchanged, and `scripts/fairness-probe.mjs` now checks each die against the hold
its own run printed. ADR-0009 carries a correction marker at the claim this replaces. The cocked
tilt held: the D10 lay flat at most 0.056° and propped at least 0.9907°, either side of 0.5°.

**Fairness, at the per-die holds** (`node scripts/fairness-probe.mjs`, every check passed, with
the D6, D8 and D20 re-tallied because their holds changed too):

| D10 run | Results | Chi-square p (lowest of 3 seeds) | On the previous face (chance 10%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tally from rest, one die | 7,000 | 0.587 | |
| Tally from rest, three dice | 7,002 | 0.059 | |
| Chained on shakes, one die | 7,000 | 0.140 | 9.90 to 10.39% |
| Chained on shakes, three dice | 7,002 | 0.631 | 9.78 to 10.15% |

Replays with the product's kick, pooled by kind of shake, came back on the previous face 9.91%
(gentle) to 10.21% (one-hand).

**On the devices** (`npm run verify:roll`, 40 rolls of three D10 on each board plus the other
three dice re-run at their new holds, 2026-11-23): every roll locked and every type passed its
chi-square. The D10's p was 0.187 on the StickC and 0.756 on the Cardputer, its longest roll
1.08 s and 1.47 s, and its four physics steps cost at most 4.4 ms and 4.7 ms, under the D6's on
both boards; no frame was late anywhere. The per-die hold showed on the hardware: the D6's mean
roll fell from 0.54 s to 0.36 s on the StickC against the same check before this slice.

One StickC D20 result out of 180 was knocked over after locking, where the run before this slice
had none. It is not the shorter D20 hold. Over 54,000 simulated three-D20 results per hold, the
knocked-over count was 182, 185 and 151 at 0.16 s against 185, 166 and 160 at 0.17 s, three seeds
each: a difference of 2 in 54,000 against a seed-to-seed spread of 34. The device's single event
is the ordinary 0.3% rate for three D20 colliding, and invariant #3 means a knocked-over die's
reported result does not change.

Both captures matched the band by eye (StickC 4, 8, 5 = 17; Cardputer 10, 4, 5 = 19), and the
numerals read on the kites, which was the one scenario no unit test could settle. The Cardputer's
became the README's D10 picture because it shows a **10**: the two-digit numeral is the widest
the product draws and the visible consequence of numbering 1 to 10, and it sits inside its kite.
